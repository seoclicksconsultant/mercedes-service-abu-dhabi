## **Why Timely Oil Changes Matter**

Heat is the biggest enemy of engine oil. As a general rule, the oxidation rate of oil [doubles with every 10°C](https://www.machinerylubrication.com/Articles/Print/32752?id=32752) rise in temperature, which roughly halves its useful life. Abu Dhabi summers, long idling in traffic and turbocharged engines all push oil temperatures higher.

Oxidised oil thickens, forms sludge and loses its protective additives. That affects timing chains, camshaft adjusters and turbochargers first, which are some of the most expensive parts to repair on a Mercedes.

Leaks are also common in the UAE. A dubizzle Cars review of over 20,000 inspections in 2026 found oil sweating in about 27% of vehicles and [active oil leaks](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/stranded-on-a-uae-road-this-summer-heres-what-the-likely-fault-could-be) in 15%. That’s why every oil change we do includes a leak check.

## **Mercedes Oil Approvals Explained**

Mercedes oils are approved by number, not just by viscosity. A 5W-30 oil may carry MB 229.5, [229.51 or 229.52](https://oilspecifications.org/articles/mercedes-benz-mb-229-oil-approvals-explained-2295-22951-22952-22971-and-22972), another carmaker’s approval, or none at all. Here is a simple guide.

| MB approval | Typical viscosity | What it’s used for |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| MB 229.5 | 0W-40, 5W-40 | Many older petrol engines |
| MB 229.51 | 5W-30 | Low SAPS oil for engines with particulate filters |
| MB 229.52 | 5W-30 | Newer Low SAPS oil for many modern petrol and diesel engines |
| MB 229.71 | 0W-20 | Low-viscosity oil for modern efficient engines |
| MB 229.72 | 0W-20 | Low-viscosity oil for M254, M256, M260, M264 and M282 petrol engines, excluding hybrids |

MB 229.71 and 229.72 are not general upgrades for engines that used 5W-30 or 5W-40 before. Each engine needs the exact approval Mercedes lists for it, which you can check on the official [Mercedes fluids database](https://operatingfluids.mercedes-benz.com/sheet/229.71/en).

## **Tips to Protect Your Mercedes Engine Oil**

* **Follow your ASSYST reminder:** Don’t ignore or reset the service message without doing the work.  
* **Check the level regularly:** Use the instrument cluster or the onboard oil level check every few weeks.  
* **Use the exact MB approval:** The right number on the label matters more than the brand name.  
* **Let the engine warm up gently:** Avoid hard revving until the oil reaches operating temperature.  
* **Limit long idling:** In heavy traffic, oil temperature rises with little airflow to cool it.  
* **Fix leaks early:** A small sweat can turn into a leak that damages wiring and engine mounts.  
* **Keep your invoices:** Detailed records protect your warranty and your resale value.
